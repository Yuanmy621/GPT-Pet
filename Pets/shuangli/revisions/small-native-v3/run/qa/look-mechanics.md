# Small native character gaze mechanics

The torso, clasped hands, skirt, feet, compact wings and attached tail remain planted at the neutral pose's shared scale and baseline. The neck and complete head lead gaze; grouped hair locks, horns, ears and the temple rosette follow the same physical head turn. The eyes retain their original lavender iris, eyelid and highlight construction.

Up: frontal head with a clearly lifted chin and eyes aimed upward. Down: frontal head with chin tucked, lowered eyes and more fringe overlapping the forehead. Right and left: visible head yaw, with nose and face surface projecting toward the named screen edge. Diagonals combine that yaw with visible upward or downward pitch; they must not become a second horizontal profile.

The silver temple rosette belongs to the character's anatomical left. It appears on viewer-right in the neutral pose. During screen-right yaw it moves to the far side and may be occluded. During screen-left yaw it remains on the visible near side. Do not flip its anatomical attachment or reintroduce it on the wrong side.

Keep the larger, readable face and cleaner outlines of the new canonical base in every direction. Avoid whole-body rotation, pupil-only sliding, replacement eyes, a head-only style change, broad warping or a changing dress template. All sixteen poses are one continuous clockwise gaze family.
